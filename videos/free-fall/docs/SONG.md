# Free Fall — background & production notes (from the *Maps & Territory* album booklet)

"Free Fall" is pure adrenaline. It's the track you play with the windows down, the one that makes you want to run.
Built on driving acoustic guitar strums, handclaps, and shimmering electric guitar delay, it has a festival-stage
energy that never relents. Where "Let It Burn" was about the grief of letting go, "Free Fall" is about the rush of
leaping forward. The bridge strips everything to just handclaps, stomps, and voice — raw, tribal, almost like a crowd
chanting — before the biggest chorus on the album erupts. The whole song should feel like running downhill with your
arms out.

## Understanding: Asymmetric Risk

Asymmetric Risk describes situations where the potential downside is small and capped, but the potential upside is
enormous and open-ended. The key insight is that most people dramatically overweight the small downside and
completely ignore the massive upside.

Consider the math of everyday asymmetric bets. Sending a cold email to someone you admire: worst case, they don't
reply (cost: two minutes and a mildly bruised ego). Best case, you gain a mentor, a job, a collaborator. Asking
someone out: worst case, a polite no. Best case, a relationship that changes your life. Publishing your writing:
worst case, nobody reads it. Best case, it reaches thousands.

Nassim Taleb formalized this as the "Barbell Strategy": put most of your resources into extremely safe positions, and
a small portion into extremely risky ones with unlimited upside. Avoid the middle, where the risk is moderate and the
reward is also moderate.

The reason most people don't take these asymmetric bets is loss aversion — the pain of a small loss feels roughly
twice as powerful as the pleasure of an equivalent gain. We focus on "what if I fail?" and forget to ask "what if it
works?" The antidote is to always evaluate both sides of the bet honestly: if the worst realistic outcome is a bruise
and the best realistic outcome is transformative, you should take that bet every single time.

Free fall isn't reckless. It's rational.
